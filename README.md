# AI for Ferroelectric Physics 🔬

**Hands-on notebooks for the IEEE Ferroschool AI Tutorials**

How can AI help us understand ferroelectric materials, explore their energy landscapes, and extract physical information from microscopy images?

These tutorials explore the intersection of AI and ferroelectric physics through interactive computational experiments. From learning Landau energy landscapes to predicting polarization maps from simulated STEM images, we will investigate how computational models can help us study physical systems.

**No prior expertise in AI is required. Bring your physics intuition and curiosity!**

## 🚀 Explore the notebooks

### 1. Learning the Landau Landscape

How does a ferroelectric energy landscape shape polarization and phase transitions?

Explore a simple Landau model, visualize its energy landscape, and investigate how changes in model parameters influence ferroelectric behavior. Then, explore how a neural network can learn patterns in the model and predict physical behavior.

**What you'll explore**
- Landau free energy, ferroelectric energy landscapes
- The relationship between model parameters, polarization, phase behavior
- How neural networks learn from simulated physical data
- What a model learns, and how its predictions connect to physical intuition

### 2. Simulate and Predict Polarization Maps with a Neural Network

Can a neural network learn to predict polarization patterns from simulated microscopy data?

Explore simulated scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) images and use a neural network to predict polarization maps. Investigate how noise and imaging conditions influence predictions, and examine how the data used to train a model affects its performance.

**What you'll explore**
- Simulating STEM images and examining their features
- Predicting polarization maps using a neural network
- Investigating the effects of image noise and window size
- Understanding how data quality and training distributions influence model predictions

## 💻 Getting started

All notebooks are available in the [`notebooks/`](./notebooks/) directory.

The easiest way to run them is using [Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/), which lets you execute Python notebooks directly in your browser without setting up a local environment.

1. Open a notebook from the `notebooks/` directory.
2. Open it in Google Colab.
3. Run the cells one at a time and explore the visualizations.
4. Change model parameters, experiment with the inputs, and observe how the results change.

Follow any setup instructions provided within the individual notebooks.

What can a learning algorithm discover about a physical system? What does it miss? And how can physical principles help us interpret what it learns?

By connecting computational results to physical intuition, we will examine not only what models can predict, but also what those predictions tell us about the underlying science.

The goal is to experiment, question, and develop a deeper understanding of both the physics and the models we use to study it.
